# The southern reversal — and a correction

Added 4 August 2026. Full detail on **The South** tab of `analysis.html`.

## Correction to an earlier finding in this project

`claude/comparative-findings.md` recorded, as "the striking result", that the Mediterranean
region moved **0.1pp** on the EU average between 1996 and 2025 while the post-communist regions
gained 27–44pp. The number is arithmetically correct and the interpretation was wrong. **It is a
fact about medians, not about the Mediterranean.**

| Country | 1996 | Peak | Year | 2025 | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Malta | 81.5 | 99.6 | 2025 | 99.6 | **+18.1** |
| Spain | 91.5 | 103.5 | 2006 | 91.8 | +0.4 |
| Portugal | 82.1 | 85.3 | 1999 | 80.2 | −1.9 |
| Cyprus | 90.1 | 106.1 | 2009 | 88.1 | −2.0 |
| Greece | 88.0 | 95.8 | 2004 | 68.0 | **−20.0** |
| *Italy** | 125.1 | 125.6 | 1998 | 96.5 | **−28.6** |

\* Italy sits in the Continental core bloc, not the Mediterranean, which is why it has been
invisible throughout this study. **It is the largest single reversal in the entire dataset** — a
founding member that fell from 25% above the EU average to 3.5% below it.

Four of the five southern members rose and then fell. Malta never stopped climbing. Averaging
that produces a flat line describing nobody. The question is not why the south stagnated; it is
why it **converged and then gave the gains back**.

## Answer one: the euro bought a decade of cheap credit

Ten-year bond yield, spread over Germany, percentage points:

| Country | 1996 | 2001 | 2007 | 2012 | 2019 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greece | +8.21 | +0.50 | +0.28 | **+21.00** | +2.84 | +0.78 |
| Spain | +2.52 | +0.32 | +0.09 | +4.35 | +0.91 | +0.62 |
| Portugal | +2.34 | +0.36 | +0.20 | +9.05 | +1.01 | +0.48 |
| Cyprus | — | +2.82 | +0.26 | +5.50 | +1.32 | +0.33 |
| Italy | +3.18 | +0.39 | +0.27 | +3.99 | +2.20 | +1.00 |

Greece paid **8.2 points over Germany in 1996 and 0.28 points in 2007**. Markets priced southern
sovereign debt as though it carried German credit risk. Every southern income peak — Portugal
1999, Greece 2004, Spain 2006, Cyprus 2009 — falls inside the window when that was running, and
the collapse coincides with the repricing.

**This is timing consistency, not proof.** Establishing causation needs the pre-crisis credit
growth and current-account data already flagged as missing in `claude/crisis-findings.md`.

## Answer two: demography ran the opposite way from the east

Growth 1996–2025 decomposed:

| Country | Population | Total real output | Output per head | Share from demography |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latvia | −24.8% | +163.4% | +250.3% | +22.7% |
| Bulgaria | −23.1% | +89.1% | +145.8% | +29.2% |
| Poland | −5.7% | +193.5% | +211.1% | +5.1% |
| Greece | −1.8% | +34.4% | +37.0% | +5.9% |
| Italy | +3.6% | +20.7% | +16.5% | −23.2% |
| Portugal | +7.4% | +50.5% | +40.2% | −21.0% |
| Spain | +23.7% | +78.3% | +44.1% | −58.3% |
| Malta | +52.6% | +322.3% | +176.7% | −41.5% |
| Cyprus | +55.7% | +169.3% | +72.9% | −80.8% |

**Cyprus and Latvia are the pair.** Over the same twenty-nine years Cyprus grew its total real
economy 169% and Latvia 163% — within six points of each other. Per head, Cyprus shows +73% and
Latvia +250%. Cyprus gained 56% more people; Latvia lost a quarter of its population. Two
economies growing at almost identical rates look, on the measure this whole study is built
around, like completely different stories.

Income per head remains the right measure for most questions — it is what a resident
experiences. But the convergence story is part economics and part demography, in both
directions, and no chart in this project has said so.

## Ruled out

- **Trade is not the differentiator.** Trade openness 1996–2025 rose 46.6pp in Greece, 54.9 in
  Malta, 48.6 in Cyprus, against 51.7 in Poland and 47.4 in Czechia. Comparable magnitudes; the
  east front-loaded around accession, the south accumulated later.
- **Unemployment does not explain persistence.** Greek unemployment went 8.4% (2007) → 27.7%
  (2013) → 8.5% (2025); Spain 8.2 → 26.1 → 10.4. The labour market largely healed. The income
  gap did not.
