# The five exclusive competences, against the three non-members

Written 6 August 2026. Article 3(1) TFEU lists five areas where **only** the Union may act:
the customs union, the competition rules necessary for the internal market, monetary policy
for euro-area states, the conservation of marine biological resources under the common
fisheries policy, and the common commercial policy.

Everything else in the acquis is shared or supporting competence, which means a non-member can
approximate it by agreement — and approximating it by agreement is exactly what the EEA and the
Swiss bilaterals do. **These five have no treaty substitute.** They are where the distance
between close association and membership actually sits, which is why they are worth separating
out from the 25-area coverage map.

A note on the scoring. Four values, not three: *cannot apply here* is not the same as *outside
it*. Switzerland has no marine fisheries competence to be outside of, and no country outside
the euro can be inside a competence that exists only for euro-area states. Collapsing those
into "outside" would overstate the distance in both directions.

| | Norway | Iceland | Switzerland |
|---|---|---|---|
| The customs union | outside it | outside it | outside it |
| Competition and state aid | **applies in full** | **applies in full** | applies in part |
| Monetary policy | cannot apply | cannot apply | cannot apply |
| Conservation of marine resources | outside it | outside it | cannot apply |
| The common commercial policy | outside it | outside it | outside it |

## Norway

**How deep in, in numbers.** EU acts incorporated into the EEA and in force: **9,164** (EFTA, EEA-Lex (retrieved 6 August 2026)). Acts adopted by the EU but not yet incorporated: **595** (EEA Council progress report, Council doc ST 1603/2026 (8 April 2026)). Of those: **** (). Open infringement cases: **28** (EFTA Surveillance Authority, Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 (30 November 2025)). Directive transposition deficit: **0.7%** (Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 — the EU average is 1.1%).

### The customs union — outside it

*Where it stands.* Outside it, and setting its own external tariff. But inside the Common Transit Convention alongside the EU27, and since the customs-security amendment of March 2021 inside the EU's Import Control System 2, which waives entry and exit summary declarations. Origin is governed by the pan-Euro-Mediterranean convention.

*Outstanding.* Origin verification, tariff classification and customs valuation still fall on every consignment crossing to the EU. No published figure exists for what that costs Norway, and none should be invented.

*Point of contention.* Not the primary friction. The customs border is administered rather than disputed.

Source: European Commission (Taxation and Customs Union); EFTA

### Competition and state aid — **applies in full**

*Where it stands.* As deep as it goes without membership. Substantively identical rules apply, enforced through the EEA's two-pillar system by the EFTA Surveillance Authority and the EFTA Court rather than by the Commission and the Court of Justice. Transposition deficit 0.7% against an EU average of 1.1%.

*Outstanding.* The revised state aid rules, which would give the Surveillance Authority direct power to obtain confidential business information and to fine Norwegian undertakings for non-cooperation.

*Point of contention.* Not which rules apply, but who enforces them and by what parliamentary majority Norway may agree to it. Opponents argue direct fining powers engage Article 115 of the Constitution and its three-quarters majority. The precedent runs the other way: on 31 October 2023 the Supreme Court held in HR-2023-2030-P that a simple majority sufficed for the ACER transfer.

Source: EFTA Surveillance Authority, Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 (30 November 2025); Supreme Court of Norway HR-2023-2030-P

### Monetary policy — cannot apply

*Where it stands.* Not applicable, and not by choice — this competence is exclusive only for states whose currency is the euro. Norges Bank sets the policy rate for the krone; it was held at 4.25% on 18 June 2026. Nothing in the EEA Agreement carries a monetary obligation.

*Outstanding.* Nothing. ERM II is an arrangement for EU member states outside the euro area, so it is structurally unavailable to a non-member rather than declined.

*Point of contention.* None. This is the one exclusive competence that generates no friction with a non-member.

Source: Norges Bank

### Conservation of marine resources — outside it

*Where it stands.* Entirely outside the common fisheries policy. Managed instead by annual bilateral consultations with the EU — the 2026 round concluded on 18–19 December 2025, with the EU receiving 9,196 tonnes of Arctic cod and transferring 47,905 tonnes of blue whiting — plus a trilateral with the EU and the UK for North Sea stocks, and a separate bilateral with Russia.

*Outstanding.* The trilateral framework agreement still does not exist. The 2026 agreed record notes the lack of progress in concluding one and commits the parties to finish by the end of 2026.

*Point of contention.* Svalbard. The cod strand was settled on 11–12 November 2024, fixing the EU's quota at 2.8274% of the total allowable catch, or 9,217 tonnes. The underlying disagreement over the 1920 Svalbard Treaty and the legality of Norway's fisheries protection zone is de-escalated, not resolved.

Source: European Commission (Oceans and Fisheries); EU–Norway–UK agreed records 2026 (Scottish Government)

### The common commercial policy — outside it

*Where it stands.* Full autonomy, exercised jointly through EFTA and bilaterally. EFTA's own dashboard records 35 free trade agreements signed with 49 countries and territories outside the EU; its FAQ page gives 31 agreements covering 42 partners in force. Neither page is dated and the two do not reconcile exactly.

*Outstanding.* Norway has no free trade agreement with China. What exists is a narrower agreement on the import and export of food including seafood, signed 21 April 2017.

*Point of contention.* Autonomy is real but has not redirected trade. Norway sends 67.5% of its goods exports to the EU — a higher share than Germany, France or Italy — after three decades of running its own trade policy.

Source: EFTA free trade dashboard and FAQ (retrieved 6 August 2026)

### The live argument in 2026: Who enforces the rules — and by what majority Norway may agree to it

The argument is not about which rules apply. Norway has taken the internal-market rulebook for thirty years. It is about enforcement powers moving to the EFTA Surveillance Authority, and whether that engages Article 115 of the Constitution and its three-quarters majority rather than a simple one. The Supreme Court answered that question for energy on 31 October 2023 and the challenge failed; the state aid rules put it again. Behind it sits the exposure: roughly 1.3 million tonnes of seafood a year go to the EU.

Source: Supreme Court of Norway HR-2023-2030-P; EFTA Surveillance Authority

## Iceland

**How deep in, in numbers.** EU acts incorporated into the EEA and in force: **9,164** (EFTA, EEA-Lex (retrieved 6 August 2026)). Acts adopted by the EU but not yet incorporated: **595** (EEA Council progress report, Council doc ST 1603/2026 (8 April 2026)). Of those: **** (). Open infringement cases: **120** (Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 — the EU average is 25). Directive transposition deficit: **1.5%** (Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 — the EU average is 1.1%).

### The customs union — outside it

*Where it stands.* Outside it, setting its own external tariff, and inside the Common Transit Convention alongside the EU27. Origin is governed by the pan-Euro-Mediterranean convention.

*Outstanding.* Origin verification, classification and valuation on every consignment. No published cost figure exists.

*Point of contention.* Low. An island with no land border faces less of the friction a customs border creates than Norway or Switzerland do.

Source: European Commission (Taxation and Customs Union); EFTA

### Competition and state aid — **applies in full**

*Where it stands.* Substantively identical rules, enforced by the EFTA Surveillance Authority and the EFTA Court rather than the Commission and the Court of Justice. But the compliance record is the weakest in the EEA: a transposition deficit of 1.5% against an EU average of 1.1%, and 120 open infringement cases against an EU average of 25.

*Outstanding.* The same revised state aid rules Norway is arguing about, and the infringement backlog itself.

*Point of contention.* The 120 open cases are the striking number on this page. Iceland already carries a larger compliance deficit than any EU member state average, in a country now asking whether to go further in.

Source: EFTA Surveillance Authority, Internal Market Scoreboard No. 57 (30 November 2025)

### Monetary policy — cannot apply

*Where it stands.* Not applicable — the competence is exclusive only for euro-area states. The Central Bank of Iceland sets the policy rate for the króna; the seven-day term deposit rate was raised to 7.75% on 20 May 2026, some 550 basis points above the ECB's deposit rate.

*Outstanding.* Nothing under the EEA. On accession Iceland would be committed in principle to adopt the euro, with no opt-out and no timetable — and Sweden has held that position since 1995 without ever entering ERM II.

*Point of contention.* Not currently contested with the EU. It becomes a live question only if accession does.

Source: Central Bank of Iceland; European Central Bank

### Conservation of marine resources — outside it

*Where it stands.* Entirely outside, and with no bilateral fisheries agreement with the EU of the kind Norway has. This is the single exclusive competence that decides the Icelandic membership question.

*Outstanding.* A comprehensive coastal-states agreement on mackerel, absent since the dispute began and still absent.

*Point of contention.* The relationship has inverted. In March 2014 the EU, Norway and the Faroes agreed a mackerel share that excluded Iceland. On 15 December 2025 a four-party arrangement between the Faroes, Iceland, Norway and the UK set a 2026 total allowable catch of 299,010 tonnes with shares of 30.55% to the UK, 26.40% to Norway, 12.00% to the Faroes and 10.50% to Iceland — and the EU is not a party to it.

Source: Coastal states agreed records 2026 (Scottish Government); European Commission

### The common commercial policy — outside it

*Where it stands.* Full autonomy through EFTA and bilaterally. Iceland signed its own free trade agreement with China on 15 April 2013, in force from 1 July 2014 — the first such agreement between China and a European country, three months ahead of Switzerland's, and the EU still has none.

*Outstanding.* Nothing outstanding. This is the competence where a small non-member has most visibly used its freedom.

*Point of contention.* On accession the China agreement would lapse into the EU's trade relationship with China, and the negotiating capacity behind it would move to Brussels. That is the most concrete single thing Iceland would give up.

Source: EFTA; European Parliament briefing PE 130631

### The live argument in 2026: Fisheries — the one competence never tested

The referendum of 29 August 2026 asks only whether to resume negotiations, and beneath it sits the exclusive competence Iceland has never tabled a position on. Chapter 13 was never opened between 2009 and 2013 because the Commission never issued the screening report. Iceland's own Foreign Ministry has put it plainly: the European Union alone holds the competence to conserve resources through its common fisheries policy.

Source: Althingi þskj. 1245; Icelandic Ministry for Foreign Affairs summary of the negotiations

## Switzerland

**How deep in, in numbers.** Bilateral agreements in force with the EU: **over 100** (Swiss Federal Council (31 October 2025). The EEAS said “more than 120” in 2021; no single authoritative count is published, so this is a range rather than a number.). New package signed: **2 March 2026** (European Commission IP/26/505). Agreements modernised and newly created: **5 and 3** (Swiss Federal Council dispatch, 13 March 2026 — new agreements on electricity, food safety and health). Ratified: **not yet** (Council of States postponed the referendum-threshold decision 24–20 on 11 June 2026; a final parliamentary decision is not expected before 2027). Referendum called: **no** (A public vote is widely expected but none has been called and no date is set).

### The customs union — outside it

*Where it stands.* Outside it, setting its own external tariff, and inside the Common Transit Convention alongside the EU27. Customs security runs through the 1990 agreement on simplification of inspections and formalities, amended in 2009 and again in March 2021 to bring Switzerland into the EU's Import Control System 2.

*Outstanding.* Origin verification, classification and valuation on every consignment — and Switzerland sits geographically in the middle of the EU, so the volume crossing that border is large. No published cost figure exists.

*Point of contention.* Administered rather than disputed. The contested Swiss questions are institutional, not tariff.

Source: European Commission (Taxation and Customs Union)

### Competition and state aid — applies in part

*Where it stands.* Shallow and sectoral. EU competition and state aid rules reach Switzerland only in air transport, under Articles 8 to 13 of the 1999 Air Transport Agreement, where the European Commission enforces them directly. Everywhere else the Competition Commission applies Swiss law. A cooperation agreement in force since 1 December 2014 lets the two authorities exchange confidential information but harmonises no substantive law.

*Outstanding.* The 2026 package would extend EU state aid disciplines to air and land transport.

*Point of contention.* Whether to accept those disciplines at all — part of the wider argument about how much EU law Switzerland takes without a vote on it.

Source: EU–Switzerland Air Transport Agreement (CELEX 22002A0430(02)); Swiss Federal Department of Foreign Affairs

### Monetary policy — cannot apply

*Where it stands.* Not applicable — the competence is exclusive only for euro-area states. The Swiss National Bank held its policy rate at 0% on 18 June 2026 and states an increased willingness to intervene in the foreign exchange market to prevent rapid franc appreciation.

*Outstanding.* Nothing. No Swiss agreement with the EU touches monetary policy.

*Point of contention.* None. The franc's strength is an economic problem for Switzerland, not a legal dispute with the Union.

Source: Swiss National Bank (18 June 2026)

### Conservation of marine resources — cannot apply

*Where it stands.* Not applicable. Switzerland is landlocked and no marine fisheries competence is engaged. It is worth stating rather than leaving blank: the exclusive competence that dominates the Norwegian and Icelandic arguments is simply absent here.

*Point of contention.* None — and its absence is part of why the Swiss argument is about institutions rather than resources.

### The common commercial policy — outside it

*Where it stands.* Full autonomy through EFTA and bilaterally, and the most active bilateral negotiator of the three. Switzerland's free trade agreement with China entered into force on 1 July 2014, the same day as Iceland's.

*Outstanding.* Nothing outstanding.

*Point of contention.* Not contested. Trade policy is the area where the Swiss arrangement most clearly outperforms membership on its own terms.

Source: EFTA; Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO)

### The live argument in 2026: The threshold for approval — not the content of the deal

The Federal Council wants an optional referendum. Parliamentary committees want a mandatory treaty referendum requiring a double majority of people and cantons, which would give small rural cantons disproportionate weight. On 11 June 2026 the Council of States declined to decide, 24–20, and sent it back to committee; a final parliamentary decision is not expected before 2027. Choosing the threshold very substantially chooses the outcome — and underneath it sits the same question that collapsed the talks in May 2021: dynamic alignment, and the role of the Court of Justice in settling disputes.

Source: Swiss Federal Council; SWI swissinfo.ch (11 June 2026)

## What could not be established to the confidence bar

**No published figure exists** for the number of customs declarations Norway or Switzerland
processes on EU trade, or for the cost of rules-of-origin compliance to any of the three. The
routes worth trying are the Swiss BAZG *Fakten und Zahlen* PDF and Norwegian Customs' annual
report; neither could be opened. Nobody should put a number here without one of those.

**The EFTA Surveillance Authority's aggregate enforcement figures** — state aid decisions taken,
competition cases opened — could not be retrieved; the authority's site is JavaScript-rendered.
The Internal Market Scoreboard figures used above are primary, dated and complete, and are the
better measure anyway.

**No average time lag** between EU adoption and EEA incorporation is published. What is published
is the composition of the backlog, which is why the figure above is "381 acts more than six
months past their compliance date" rather than an average.

**Whether the Court of Justice's interpretation would bind the arbitral tribunal** under the
Swiss package of 2 March 2026 could not be confirmed from a primary source. This is the most
consequential open item in the Swiss file, because it is the question the 2021 talks died on.
Nothing is asserted about it here.

**The snow crab dispute** has no verified 2026 development. The last firm datapoint remains the
Norwegian Supreme Court ruling of February 2019.

**Norway's absence of a China free trade agreement** rests on absence of evidence rather than a
positive source, so the page states the 2017 food and seafood agreement that does exist rather
than asserting the negative.

**EFTA's own two pages disagree** on its trade agreement count — the dashboard gives 35 signed
with 49 countries and territories, the FAQ gives 31 agreements covering 42 partners in force.
Neither page carries a date. Both are quoted rather than reconciled.
