EU membership impact

What has EU membership done to its member states — legally, financially, commercially, politically and socially — measured against comparable countries that never joined.

Central finding — income effect on Eastern members, adjusted for catch-up growth
not distinguishable from zero

Central estimate -0.6%, 95% interval -7.3% to +6.1%, across 11 countries. Eastern members grew enormously after joining — but so did the Western Balkan countries that did not join, and poorer economies grow faster mechanically regardless of membership.

This is an inconclusive result, not a null one. Two biases push the estimate toward zero: the control countries are all EU candidates already reforming in anticipation, and the heaviest accession reforms happen before entry, inside the pre-accession baseline. One bias pushes the other way: countries joined because they already qualified. They do not cancel in any quantifiable way.

Central finding — trade openness, Eastern members vs Western Balkan non-members
+20.5 pp of GDP

95% interval +14.3 to +26.7 percentage points, and all 11 of 11 Eastern members are above their control group — there is no country carrying this average on its own. Run entirely on pre-accession years the same estimator reports +0.6, so the gap opens at accession rather than before it.

This is the single result in the study that survives every check applied to it, and it is worth being precise about what it claims. Trade is the channel the single market acts on most directly, so an effect here is what the theory predicts. It does not follow that people became better off: that is the income question above, and the income question remains unresolved.

Three ways in

The project has three layers. Start with whichever question you have.

The argument → Does membership change a country's trajectory? Event-time alignment, bloc splits, difference-in-differences against non-members, and an explicit account of what the method cannot show. Country comparison → Where every member stands today on income, GNI, unemployment and convergence with the EU average — ranked, plus each country's full trajectory. A worked country page → Poland is the completed reference: five lenses, verified milestone timeline, and written analysis. Every other country has the same charts and timeline with narrative pending.
